Florida's oldest restaurants

17 Florida restaurants, bars and lunch counters open at their current address since 1960 or earlier, oldest first, each checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. 12 opened before 1950. The oldest, Columbia Restaurant in Tampa, dates from 1905. Under each one, a note says what the year rests on.

How a founding year is chosen

A founding year here means the year the place opened at this address in its present form, as the place itself or a published history documents it. It is never the year a brand started elsewhere, and never a year before a move. When sources disagree we use the later date. Several places call themselves the oldest in their town or county, and one calls itself Florida's oldest restaurant; those are their own claims, and the note under each place attributes them as written. A place that isn't here may simply not have been documented yet: email us with a source.

Oldest first

  1. Columbia Restaurant

    2117 E 7th Ave, Tampa

    Cuban & cafecito On the menu: Cuban sandwich (house-baked ham, mojo pork, Genoa salami, Swiss, pickles, mustard on La Segunda bread), Spanish-Cuban plates, flamenco dinner show · Since 1905

    Block-long Spanish-Cuban dining hall in Ybor City, run by the founding family and known for its tiled rooms, flamenco show and Tampa-style Cuban.

    The restaurant says Casimiro Hernandez Sr. founded it in 1905 in a 1903 corner saloon that is part of today's block-long Ybor building; it calls itself "Florida's Oldest Restaurant℠" (its service mark, an attributed claim).

  2. Joe's Stone Crab

    11 Washington Ave, Miami Beach

    MICHELIN RecommendedAmerica's ClassicStone crabKeys classic On the menu: stone crab claws, mustard sauce, hash browns, creamed spinach, fried chicken, Key lime pie · Since 1918 · Season: Seasonally closed Sept 14 – Oct 16, 2026 (reopens Oct 16 for its 114th season); stone crab claws Oct 15 – May 1 (FWC season).

    South Pointe institution where stone crabs were first served chilled and cracked in the 1920s, run by the Weiss family's fourth generation; Joe's Take Away next door sells claws to go.

    Joe Weiss opened a lunch counter at Smith's bathing casino in 1913; in 1918 he and Jennie bought a bungalow on Biscayne Street and opened Joe's Restaurant, and the restaurant's history says it still operates in the family's house. The tourism bureau calls it Miami Beach's oldest restaurant.

    joesstonecrab.com

  3. Bradley's Country Store

    10655 Centerville Road, Tallahassee

    On the menu: homemade smoked sausage, sausage dog, country milled grits · Since 1927

    Family-run country store on a canopy road north of Tallahassee that still makes its sausage and grits on site and sells sausage dogs; a store, not a sit-down restaurant.

    Visit Tallahassee says the family store has operated at this same location since 1927; its own site says "since 1927." It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

    bradleyscountrystore.com

  4. Cap's Place Island Restaurant

    2765 NE 28th Ct, Lighthouse Point

    Fish camp On the menu: house-smoked fish dip, fresh local fish, Okeechobee hearts of palm salad, raw oysters, steaks · Since 1928

    Former Prohibition-era speakeasy and casino on an island off Lighthouse Point, reached by boat from Cap's dock, serving local fish and house-smoked fish dip.

    Captain Theodore Knight moved his operation to the present site in 1928, building on a beached barge; it calls itself Broward County's oldest restaurant and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places (1990). The address is Cap's dock; the restaurant sits on an island reached by the restaurant's launch.

    capsplace.com

  5. Reececliff Family Diner

    940 Florida Ave S, Lakeland

    On the menu: house-made pies, Southern breakfasts, daily specials · Since 1934

    Lakeland diner on South Florida Avenue known for Southern comfort food and pies, open daily with shorter Sunday hours.

    The diner says it has served Lakeland since 1934 from its location on South Florida Avenue and calls itself Lakeland's oldest restaurant (its own claim).

    reececliff.com

  6. No Name Pub

    30813 Watson Blvd, Big Pine Key

    Keys classicFish camp On the menu: pizza, fish dip · Since 1936

    Big Pine Key pub known for its pizza and fish dip, in a building that started as a 1931 general store.

    The pub's own history says the building opened in 1931 as a general store and bait shop, and the owners added a restaurant room to it in 1936.

    nonamepub.com

  7. Sloppy Joe's Bar

    201 Duval St, Key West

    Keys classicFish camp On the menu: conch fritters with Key lime mustard, island smoked fish dip, the original Sloppy Joe sandwich, Key lime pie · Since 1937

    Duval Street landmark bar with live music daily and a kitchen serving conch fritters, smoked fish dip and Key lime pie.

    The bar dates its history to the end of Prohibition in 1933 and says it has been at the corner of Duval and Greene since 1937.

    sloppyjoes.com

  8. The Lodge at Wakulla Springs

    550 Wakulla Park Dr, Crawfordville

    On the menu: fried shrimp, crab cakes, shrimp and grits, soda fountain · Since 1937

    Marble-and-tile 1937 lodge on the spring in Wakulla Springs State Park with a dining room and an old-fashioned soda fountain.

    Edward Ball opened the lodge in 1937 per Visit Wakulla; the restaurant and soda fountain are inside the original lodge building in the state park.

  9. Ocean Grill

    1050 Beachland Blvd, Vero Beach

    On the menu: seafood, steaks · Since 1941

    Waldo Sexton-built oceanfront dining room in Vero Beach that has survived hurricanes since 1941, now run by the second and third generation of the Replogle family.

    Its own history page says Waldo Sexton built it on the dune here and the first meal was served on New Year's Eve 1941; it served as a naval officers' club during WWII and the Replogle family has run it since 1965.

    ocean-grill.com

  10. Green Turtle Inn

    81219 Overseas Hwy, Islamorada

    Keys classicFish camp On the menu: conch chowder, conch fritters, turtle chowder (traditional red chowder), house smoked fish dip, barbecue · Since 1947

    Mile marker 81.2 roadhouse dating to 1947, serving conch chowder, conch fritters and house smoked fish dip, with a roadside barbecue stand.

    The restaurant says it has been an Islamorada dining favorite since 1947 and a landmark on the Overseas Highway at mile marker 81.2 for over 75 years.

    greenturtleinn.com

  11. Dreamette

    3646 Post St, Jacksonville

    On the menu: soft-serve ice cream, dipped cones, frozen yogurt · Since 1948

    Cash-only Murray Hill walk-up ice cream window serving cones since the late 1940s.

    Visit Jacksonville says this Post Street walk-up has served ice cream since 1948; that year comes from the tourism listing, not an independent history.

  12. Tropical Acres Steakhouse

    2500 Griffin Rd, Fort Lauderdale

    On the menu: steaks, prime rib, lobster · Since 1949

    Family-run steakhouse on Griffin Road since 1949, with an on-site butcher shop; the last of what was once a small chain of Tropical Acres restaurants.

    Gene Harvey converted an old frame house on Griffin Road into the original Tropical Acres in 1949; it was gutted by fire on April 1, 1964 and reopened on the same site that November.

    tropicalacres.com

  13. Lee & Rick's Oyster Bar

    5621 Old Winter Garden Rd, Orlando

    Oyster bar On the menu: raw oysters, steamed oyster buckets (bar only) · Since 1950

    Orlando's long-running family oyster bar, now run by the founders' oldest son, where oysters are shucked to order along an 80-foot concrete bar.

    The family's history page says Lee & Rick's opened in 1950 as a nine-stool oyster bar, with the founders living in the back of the building; it describes growth to today's 80-foot shucking bar and mentions no move.

    leeandricksoysterbar.com

  14. Ted Peters' Famous Smoked Fish

    1350 Pasadena Ave S, St. Petersburg

    Fish camp On the menu: smoked mullet, smoked Spanish mackerel, smoked fish spread, German potato salad · Since 1951

    Open-air smokehouse restaurant on Pasadena Avenue in South Pasadena (St. Petersburg mailing address) that smokes mullet, mackerel and other fish over red oak, still in the founding families' hands; closed Mondays and Tuesdays.

    The restaurant's history page says it was running at its current location by 1951, and the county tourism bureau says it has been in the same rustic building in South Pasadena since 1951.

    tedpetersfish.com

  15. Bern's Steak House

    1208 S Howard Ave, Tampa

    MICHELIN Recommended On the menu: dry-aged steaks, dessert room, wine list · Since 1956

    South Tampa steakhouse famous for its huge wine cellar and upstairs Harry Waugh Dessert Room, with wine lists dated 2026.

    Bern's says it was founded in 1956 and created from a business block on Howard Avenue that held a grocery, barber shop and dime store; it marks its 70th anniversary in 2026.

    bernssteakhouse.com

  16. Jaxson's Ice Cream Parlor & Restaurant

    128 S Federal Hwy, Dania Beach

    On the menu: house-made ice cream, sundaes, classic diner fare · Since 1956

    Family-owned ice cream parlor on Federal Highway making 50-plus flavors on the premises daily, with walls full of license plates and antiques.

    Monroe Udell opened Jaxson's at its present Federal Highway location, just north of Stirling Road, in 1956; the family still owns it.

    jaxsonsicecream.com

  17. Columbia Restaurant

    411 St Armands Cir, Sarasota

    Cuban & cafecito On the menu: Cuban sandwich, Spanish-Cuban plates · Since 1959

    St. Armands Circle branch of the Ybor City Spanish-Cuban restaurant family, open since 1959 and listing regular daily hours after the 2024 storm flooding on the Circle.

    Bay News 9 (July 2025) reports this St. Armands Circle restaurant opened in December 1959, the Columbia family's first expansion beyond Ybor, and calls it Sarasota's oldest restaurant; no move is reported.

Old Florida on the water

Fish camps started as bait shops and boat ramps on rivers, lakes and the Intracoastal, and many grew kitchens: fried catfish and mullet, shrimp, hush puppies and a view of the water. Smoked fish dip, made from smoked mullet, mackerel or other local fish, is the snack of both coasts.

What's on the list

Towns with the most: Key West (2), Islamorada (2), St. Petersburg (2), St. Augustine (2), New Smyrna Beach (2), Jacksonville (2), Crawfordville (2), Lighthouse Point (1). The dishes we found most often: smoked fish dip (9), raw oysters (5), key lime pie (4), conch chowder (4), conch fritters (4), smoked fish spread (3).

How the list was built: each place was checked against a 2025 or 2026 source, such as its own website or menu, recent local news or an official tourism listing, with the address checked. Places with no current source or a dead website were left out. Nothing here comes from review sites, and the order is by region and town, not by anyone's rating.
